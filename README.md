# Draft Your First Spec

_Turn an unfamiliar business problem into a validated OpenSpec change, and decide honestly whether it is ready to build._

## Welcome

- **Who is this for**: Product owners, business analysts, testers, developers and architects who can read an OpenSpec change and Given/When/Then scenarios, ideally after exercises 1 to 3 of this workshop.
- **What you'll learn**: How to frame a change in a proposal, write requirements and edge-case scenarios that pass strict validation, and make a reasoned Ready to build decision with measures, using Copilot as a drafting partner while you make the decisions.
- **What you'll build**: A new OpenSpec change with a proposal, a spec delta with at least three scenarios and a readiness record, shared in a pull request.
- **Prerequisites**:
  - Comfort with branches, commits and pull requests. If they are new to you, start with [Introduction to GitHub](https://github.com/skills/introduction-to-github).
  - Access to [GitHub Codespaces](https://docs.github.com/en/codespaces/overview). Personal accounts include monthly free usage.
  - [GitHub Copilot](https://github.com/features/copilot) with Copilot Chat. Copilot Free is enough.
- **How long**: This exercise takes about 60 minutes to complete.

In this exercise, you will:

1. Write an OpenSpec proposal that states why a change matters, what changes and what it will not do.
1. Write requirements and at least three Given/When/Then scenarios, including edge cases, that pass strict validation.
1. Record capability confirmations, edge cases, measures and a ready or not ready decision, then open a pull request.

This is exercise 4 of 4 in the **Spec-Driven Delivery with OpenSpec, Gherkin and GitHub Copilot** workshop. All organizations, people and data in it are fictional.
